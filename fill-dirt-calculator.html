Quick answer

A 15 ft × 20 ft low spot averaging 6 inches deep needs 5.56 cubic yards of fill dirt — about 6.75 tons. With the 10% settling buffer, order about 6.11 cubic yards (7.43 tons), spread slightly crowned so it finishes level.

  • The math: volume = length × width × depth (depth in feet); cubic yards = cubic feet ÷ 27; tons = cubic feet × density ÷ 2,000.
  • Density used: 90 lb per cubic foot (1.22 tons per cubic yard) for mineral fill dirt — denser than topsoil, lighter than stone.
  • Bag counts are rounded up and given for both standard bag sizes — 0.75 cu ft and 1.5 cu ft (one cubic yard = 36 bags of 0.75 cu ft, or 18 bags of 1.5 cu ft).
  • The recommended order adds 10% on top of the calculated volume for settling, compaction and spills.

How to calculate fill dirt

Fill dirt jobs are grading jobs, so the key input is average depth. For a low area, drive stakes at the corners, run a level string, and measure the depth at several points — then average them. A spot that is 2 inches low at the edges and 10 inches low in the middle needs the average (about 6 inches), not the maximum. Volume = area × average depth; divide by 27 for cubic yards and multiply cubic feet by about 90 lb for tons.

Fill behaves differently from topsoil in one important way: compaction. Loose fill placed in a hole will settle 10–15% as it is compacted and rained on — more if you dump it all in at once without tamping. Pros place fill in 4–6 inch layers and compact each one. The 10% buffer in the recommended order covers normal settling for shallow fills; for holes deeper than about 12 inches, add 15–20% and plan to crown the fill slightly above grade so it settles level.

Finally, fill dirt is not a growing medium. It is the right product for raising grade, filling holes and building up under patios or sheds, but anything you plan to plant needs 4–6 inches of topsoil on top of the fill. If your project needs both, calculate the fill to a few inches below final grade and use the topsoil calculator for the top layer — the second-area option lets you do both layers in one pass here as a rough combined volume.

Worked example: 15 ft × 20 ft low spot, average 6 inches deep

A 15 ft × 20 ft low area is 300 sq ft. At an average depth of 6 inches (0.5 ft) the fill volume is 300 × 0.5 = 150 cubic feet = 5.56 cubic yards. At 90 lb per cubic foot that is 13,500 lb ≈ 6.75 tons. With normal settling, order about 6.1 cubic yards (7.43 tons with the 10% buffer), spread it slightly crowned, and expect it to finish level after a few rains.

Common mistakes

  • Using the deepest measurement everywhere. Depth at the center of a swale is not the average depth. Using it across the whole area can double your order — and leave you with a pile of leftover dirt to move.
  • Dumping deep fill in one lift. Uncompacted fill keeps settling for months, taking patios and shed floors with it. Fill in layers and tamp or roll each one.
  • Planting directly into fill dirt. Subsoil lacks organic matter and structure; grass and beds struggle. Cap fill with 4–6 inches of topsoil anywhere plants will grow.
  • Ignoring drainage slope. Grade next to a house should fall away about 6 inches over the first 10 feet. Filling a low spot flat can redirect water toward the foundation instead of away from it.

Frequently asked questions

How much fill dirt do I need to level a yard?

It depends entirely on average depth. Example: 1,000 sq ft averaging 3 inches low needs 250 cu ft ≈ 9.3 cu yd. Measure depth at several points and average — never guess from the deepest spot.

How much does a cubic yard of fill dirt weigh?

About 2,400 lb (1.2 tons) in place at the 90 lb/cu ft average used here. Very rocky or clay-heavy fill can run heavier; sandy fill a little lighter.

What is the difference between fill dirt and topsoil?

Fill dirt is subsoil — mineral soil dug from below the surface, low in organic matter, used for raising and leveling grade because it compacts stably. Topsoil is the dark, organic top layer plants grow in. Use fill for shape, topsoil for growing.

How much extra fill dirt should I order for compaction?

About 10% for shallow, hand-tamped fills (built into the recommended order here) and 15–20% for deep fills over 12 inches, especially if you cannot compact in layers.

Can I use fill dirt under a patio or shed?

Yes — compacted fill dirt is a standard sub-base. Compact in 4-inch layers, then add your gravel base on top. Uncompacted fill is the mistake; the material itself is fine.
