How to calculate gravel — and why tons matter

Start with volume as usual: area × depth, divided by 27 gives cubic yards. But most gravel yards price by the ton, so the second step is converting volume to weight with the stone's density. The quick industry rule is about 1.35 tons per cubic yard for crushed stone; this calculator uses per-stone densities (100 lb/cu ft crushed, 96 pea gravel, 90 river rock) so your tonnage estimate matches the stone you are actually buying. If a supplier quotes a different yards-to-tons conversion, type it into the density fields and everything updates.

Depth depends on the job. A gravel driveway typically needs 4–6 inches total, ideally in layers: a coarse base layer, a middle layer, and a finer top layer. A simple path or patio base takes 2–4 inches (plus sand or screenings on top for pavers). Drainage trenches and French drains are usually filled to within a few inches of the surface — measure the trench itself, and use the second-area option if the trench has an L or T shape.

Order with a buffer. Gravel compacts under traffic — a freshly spread 4-inch layer may settle to 3 inches — and some always spills outside the edges. The recommended order here adds 10%. Also check your supplier's minimum: many yards deliver from 1 ton up, and a “yard” of gravel (about 1.35 tons) is a common minimum load, so for small jobs a few dozen bags can be simpler even if the per-ton price looks worse.

Worked example: 12 ft × 40 ft driveway, 4 inches deep

A 12 ft × 40 ft driveway is 480 sq ft. At 4 inches (0.33 ft) deep the volume is 480 × 0.33 = 160 cubic feet = 5.93 cubic yards. Crushed stone at 100 lb per cubic foot weighs 160 × 100 = 16,000 lb = 8.00 tons. With the 10% compaction buffer, order about 8.80 tons (6.52 cu yd). Note how the same area in pea gravel would weigh slightly less — 7.68 tons before the buffer — because of the lower density.

Common mistakes

  • Ordering by the yard when the quote is by the ton. A “yard” of gravel is a volume (27 cu ft); a ton is weight. At 1.35 tons per yard they differ by 35% — enough to blow a budget or strand a half-finished driveway.
  • One layer, full depth, all at once. Deep single layers of fine gravel migrate and rut. Compact in lifts of 2–3 inches, or build distinct base and top layers.
  • Using round stone on a driveway. Pea gravel and river rock roll under tires and feet instead of locking. Driveways and structural bases need angular crushed stone; save round stone for decorative, low-traffic areas.
  • Forgetting the fabric and edging. Gravel spread straight onto soil slowly sinks into it. Landscape fabric plus rigid edging keeps your calculated depth where you put it.

Frequently asked questions

How many tons are in a cubic yard of gravel?

About 1.35 tons for crushed stone, 1.30 for pea gravel and 1.22 for river rock at the densities used here. Suppliers' conversions vary a little with stone type and moisture — ask yours if their number differs and enter it above.

How deep should a gravel driveway be?

Four to six inches in total. A common build is 3–4 inches of coarse base stone plus 2–3 inches of finer top stone, each compacted. This calculator's total-depth approach works if you enter the combined depth.

How much gravel do I need for a 100 sq ft area?

At 2 inches deep: 16.7 cu ft ≈ 0.62 cu yd ≈ 0.83 tons of crushed stone. At 4 inches deep, double it: about 1.23 cu yd ≈ 1.67 tons.

What is the difference between gravel, crushed stone and pea gravel?

“Gravel” is the umbrella term. Crushed stone is mechanically crushed, angular rock that compacts and locks — best for driveways and bases. Pea gravel is small, naturally rounded stone about ⅜ inch across — comfortable underfoot, poor at locking. River rock is larger rounded stone used decoratively.

How many bags of gravel make a ton?

A typical 0.5 cu ft bag of gravel weighs about 50 lb, so roughly 40 bags make a ton. At that rate bags only make sense for small patches — anything over half a ton is usually cheaper delivered in bulk.
