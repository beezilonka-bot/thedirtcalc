How to calculate mulch

The math is the same as for any ground cover: volume = area × depth, then convert cubic feet to cubic yards by dividing by 27. The part people get wrong is depth. For most flower and shrub beds, 3 inches is the working standard: enough to suppress weeds and hold moisture, not so much that it suffocates roots or piles up against siding and trunks. Around trees, 2–4 inches in a wide ring (kept a few inches away from the trunk) is right; new beds over bare soil can take 4 inches the first year.

Bag math is where mulch shopping gets confusing, because bag sizes differ. One cubic yard equals 27 cubic feet, so it takes 36 bags of 0.75 cu ft, 18 bags of 1.5 cu ft, or about 14 bags of 2 cu ft to make a yard. This calculator shows the two most common sizes side by side. As a rule of thumb, once a project passes about 2 cubic yards — roughly 36 of the 1.5 cu ft bags — bulk delivery is usually cheaper and far less plastic to throw away; our bulk vs. bagged calculator runs the exact numbers for your local prices.

Mulch also shrinks. Fresh shredded mulch settles and decomposes, losing visible depth over a season, which is why the recommended order here adds 10%. If you are topping up existing mulch rather than starting bare, measure what is already there and only add the difference — a bed with 1½ inches down needs 1½ inches more, not a full 3.

Worked example: 24 ft × 4 ft flower bed, 3 inches deep

A 24 ft × 4 ft bed covers 96 sq ft. At 3 inches (0.25 ft) deep, the volume is 96 × 0.25 = 24 cubic feet, or 0.89 cubic yards. That is exactly 16 bags of 1.5 cu ft (24 ÷ 1.5), or 32 bags of 0.75 cu ft. The mulch itself weighs only about 600 lb, so bags are easy to handle for a job this size. With the 10% settling buffer, buy 18 bags — or round a bulk order to 1 full yard.

Common mistakes

  • Mulching 2 inches deep to save money. Thin mulch lets light reach weed seeds and dries out fast. You end up re-mulching mid-season and spending more, not less.
  • Piling mulch against trunks and siding. “Volcano mulching” traps moisture against bark and invites rot and pests. Keep mulch 3–6 inches away from trunks and below the top of your foundation.
  • Comparing bag prices without checking bag size. A $3 bag at 0.75 cu ft costs more per yard than a $4.50 bag at 2 cu ft. Always compare price per cubic foot.
  • Ordering for bare ground when topping up. Measure existing depth first. Topping up usually needs half the volume of a fresh bed.

Frequently asked questions

How many bags of mulch are in a cubic yard?

It depends on bag size: 36 bags at 0.75 cu ft, 18 bags at 1.5 cu ft, or 13.5 bags at 2 cu ft make one cubic yard (27 cu ft).

How deep should mulch be?

Three inches for most beds, 2–4 inches around trees, and about 4 inches for a brand-new bed over bare soil or a dedicated weed-suppression layer over landscape fabric.

How much area does one bag of mulch cover?

A 1.5 cu ft bag covers about 6 sq ft at 3 inches deep, or 9 sq ft at 2 inches. A 2 cu ft bag covers about 8 sq ft at 3 inches.

Is bulk mulch cheaper than bagged?

Usually, past roughly 2 cubic yards — but delivery fees change the answer for small loads. Run your local prices through the bulk vs. bagged calculator on this site for an exact comparison.

How much does a cubic yard of mulch weigh?

Roughly 400–800 lb depending on material and moisture — about 675 lb at the 25 lb/cu ft average this calculator uses. Much lighter than soil or stone, which is why a yard of mulch in a pickup is reasonable while a yard of topsoil often is not.
