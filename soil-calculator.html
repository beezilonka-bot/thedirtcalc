How much soil do you need?

Start with the same formula every soil job uses: volume = length × width × depth, with depth in feet, divided by 27 for cubic yards. The real decision is the depth, and it depends on the job. A new flower bed or vegetable area worked into existing ground usually wants about 6 inches of added or improved soil. Building soil up from scratch over poor subsoil takes 8–12 inches. Refreshing an existing bed takes just 2–3 inches of new soil or compost on top. If you are filling a framed raised bed, you will get a better answer from the raised bed calculator, which starts from standard bed sizes; this page is for soil spread on or worked into the ground itself.

“Soil” on a bag or a supplier's price list can mean several different products, and picking the right one matters more than the last few percent of volume. Topsoil is screened mineral soil — the base layer, cheap and fine for levelling and general beds. Garden soil is a blend of topsoil and compost sold for in-ground beds; it costs more and grows better. Potting mix is a soilless blend for containers — light, sterile, and far too expensive to spread on the ground. For most in-ground projects the economical pattern is topsoil for depth plus a few inches of compost mixed in, rather than all premium garden soil.

Soil is sold by the cubic yard in bulk and by the cubic foot in bags — 1, 1.5 and 2 cu ft bags are all common for soil products, so check the label before comparing our bag counts (given at 0.75 and 1.5 cu ft, the standard sizes this site uses) against a shelf price. One more habit worth copying from landscapers: soil settles. Freshly spread soil compacts roughly 10% after watering, which is why the recommended order below adds it — measuring after the first good rain and finding the bed an inch low is the classic outcome of ordering the exact number.

Worked example: a new 10 ft × 20 ft flower bed, 6 inches of soil

A 10 ft × 20 ft bed is 200 sq ft. At 6 inches (0.5 ft) deep, the volume is 200 × 0.5 = 100 cubic feet, which is 3.70 cubic yards. At 75 lb per cubic foot that soil weighs about 7,500 lb, or 3.75 tons. Bagged, it would take 67 of the 1.5 cu ft bags — right at the crossover where a bulk delivery usually beats bags on price and is far less plastic to throw away. With the 10% settling buffer, the recommended order is 4.07 cubic yards, or about 4.13 tons.

Common mistakes

  • Buying potting mix for in-ground beds. Potting mix is engineered for containers — it drains too fast in open ground and costs several times more per cubic foot. In-ground beds want topsoil or garden soil.
  • One depth for every job. Six inches is a starting point, not a law: 2–3 inches refreshes an old bed, while starting a garden on hard subsoil can take a foot. Match depth to the job before you multiply.
  • Comparing bag prices without reading bag size. Soil bags come in 1, 1.5 and 2 cu ft (and 0.75 at some stores). A “cheaper” bag that holds half as much is not cheaper.
  • Spreading soil over unprepared ground. New soil dumped on compacted, weedy ground roots poorly and washes. Loosen or strip the surface first, then spread — the volume math assumes the soil stays where you put it.

Frequently asked questions

How much soil do I need for a garden bed?

Multiply the bed's length × width × the depth you want, in feet, and divide by 27 for cubic yards. A 10 × 20 ft bed at 6 inches deep needs 3.70 cubic yards. Add about 10% because fresh soil settles after watering.

What is the difference between topsoil, garden soil and potting mix?

Topsoil is screened mineral soil — the economical base. Garden soil is topsoil blended with compost for in-ground planting. Potting mix contains no real soil at all; it is a light, sterile blend made for pots and should not be spread on beds.

How many bags of soil are in a cubic yard?

A cubic yard is 27 cubic feet, so 27 bags at 1 cu ft, 18 bags at 1.5 cu ft, or about 14 bags at 2 cu ft. This calculator reports the 0.75 and 1.5 cu ft sizes; scale from the cubic-feet result for other bag sizes.

How much does a cubic yard of soil weigh?

About a ton — roughly 2,025 lb at the 75 lb per cubic foot average this calculator uses for screened soil and soil blends. Wet soil can be 10–20% heavier, which matters if you are hauling it yourself.

How deep should soil be in a flower bed?

Around 6 inches of good soil over loosened native ground suits most flowers and vegetables. Refreshing an established bed needs only 2–3 inches; starting from hard, poor subsoil may take 8–12 inches plus compost worked in.
