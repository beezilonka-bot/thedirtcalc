Quick answer

A 6 ft × 6 ft sandbox filled 6 inches deep needs 0.67 cubic yards of sand — 0.90 tons, which is 12 bags of 1.5 cu ft (24 of 0.75 cu ft), or about 36 standard 50 lb bags of play sand. With the 10% buffer, plan on 0.73 cubic yards.

  • The math: volume = length × width × depth (depth in feet); cubic yards = cubic feet ÷ 27; tons = cubic feet × density ÷ 2,000.
  • Density used: 100 lb per cubic foot (1.35 tons per cubic yard) for dry sand; wet sand runs 15–25% heavier.
  • Bag counts are rounded up and given for both standard bag sizes — 0.75 cu ft and 1.5 cu ft (one cubic yard = 36 bags of 0.75 cu ft, or 18 bags of 1.5 cu ft).
  • The recommended order adds 10% on top of the calculated volume for settling, compaction and spills.

How to calculate sand

The formula never changes: area × depth, divided by 27 for cubic yards, then multiply cubic feet by about 100 lb to get weight in tons. What changes between projects is the depth. A paver patio wants about 1 inch of bedding sand over a compacted gravel base. A sandbox is usually filled 4–8 inches deep (roughly half the box height, so sand stays in the box). Under an above-ground pool, 2–3 inches of mason sand makes the leveling layer. Leveling a low lawn area takes ¼–½ inch of topdressing sand worked into the grass.

For bag math, remember that hardware-store sand is often sold by weight, not volume: the standard 50 lb bag holds about 0.5 cubic feet. This calculator reports bag counts for the 0.75 and 1.5 cu ft sizes used by bagged soil and aggregate brands, and the tonnage lets you convert to 50 lb bags directly — one ton is forty 50 lb bags. Anything beyond a sandbox or a small paver project is almost always cheaper and easier as a bulk ton from a landscape yard.

Sand also compacts and migrates. Bedding sand gets screeded and then settles into the gravel and the paver joints; sandbox sand escapes on shoes and through drainage. The recommended figure adds 10%, and for paver joints specifically, plan a small extra bag of jointing sand for the top-up pass a few weeks after installation.

Worked example: 6 ft × 6 ft sandbox, 6 inches deep

A 6 ft × 6 ft sandbox filled 6 inches (0.5 ft) deep needs 36 × 0.5 = 18 cubic feet of sand = 0.67 cubic yards. At 100 lb per cubic foot that is 1,800 lb, or 0.90 tons — about 36 standard 50 lb bags of play sand. That is a manageable bag job; compare with the same sandbox at 8 inches deep (1.2 tons, 48 bags) before deciding to fill it to the brim.

Common mistakes

  • Filling a sandbox to the top. Sand at rim height leaves on shoes and wind within weeks. Half to two-thirds full is the practical fill line — and much cheaper.
  • Using bedding sand at base depth. Paver bedding sand is a 1-inch leveling layer, not a structural base. The 4–6 inches underneath should be compacted gravel; sand alone will shift and the pavers will rock.
  • Buying wet sand by the ton for a volume job. You pay for the water. If the pile at the yard is soaked, expect up to a fifth less coverage per ton than the calculator shows.
  • Forgetting joint sand on paver jobs. Bedding sand and jointing (polymeric) sand are different products with different jobs. Budget for both.

Frequently asked questions

How many 50 lb bags of sand make a ton?

Exactly 40 bags by weight. By volume, a 50 lb bag of dry sand is about 0.5 cu ft, so one cubic yard (27 cu ft) is roughly 54 bags — which is why bulk wins quickly.

How much sand do I need under pavers?

About 1 inch of bedding sand over the compacted base. For a 100 sq ft patio that is 8.3 cu ft ≈ 0.31 cu yd ≈ 0.42 tons.

How much sand does a sandbox need?

It depends on fill depth: a 4×4 ft box at 6 inches takes 8 cu ft (about 16 × 50 lb bags); a 6×6 at 6 inches takes 18 cu ft (about 36 bags). Use the calculator with your box size and a 4–8 inch depth.

What kind of sand should I buy?

Play sand (washed, screened) for sandboxes; mason sand for pool bases and general leveling; coarse concrete sand for paver bedding; polymeric sand for paver joints. Density is similar for all of them, so this calculator works for each.

How much does a cubic yard of sand weigh?

About 2,700 lb (1.35 tons) dry. Wet sand can exceed 3,000 lb per yard — one more reason a “ton” is not a fixed volume.
