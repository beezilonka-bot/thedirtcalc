How to calculate crushed stone

Crushed stone is quarried rock mechanically broken into angular pieces, and unlike the rounded stone in the gravel family it locks together when compacted — which is why it is the default material under driveways, pavers, sheds and slabs. The volume formula is the site standard: length × width × depth ÷ 27 = cubic yards, and at 100 lb per cubic foot each yard weighs about 1.35 tons. Most stone yards price by the ton, so the tonnage figure is the one to take to the supplier; the yardage is what to check against your truck or their delivery minimum.

Depth is a design decision, not a guess. A gravel driveway over firm ground is typically built as 4 inches of large base stone, sometimes with a 2–3 inch finer top layer — calculate each layer separately and add the results (the second-area option handles the common two-section driveway). A paver or shed base usually takes 4–6 inches of compacted crushed stone (often sold as crusher run, 3/4″ minus, or road base) beneath the bedding layer. A French drain trench is a volume problem in disguise: trench length × width × the stone-filled depth, minus a little for the pipe itself.

Stone size names are the other source of confusion. The numbers refer to a standard grading sieve series: #57 is a clean, roughly 1-inch stone that drains freely and is the usual French drain and driveway-top choice; #411 blends #57 with stone dust so it compacts — a good driveway surface; crusher run / 3/4″ minus includes fines and packs hardest, making it the base material. Clean stone does not compact much (it is already as tight as it gets once settled), while minus material shrinks about 10% under a plate compactor — the recommended order's 10% buffer covers that compaction and the inevitable spillage along the edges.

Worked example: a 12 ft × 40 ft driveway, 4 inches of crushed stone

A 12 ft × 40 ft driveway is 480 sq ft. At 4 inches (0.33 ft) deep, the volume is 480 × 0.33 = 160 cubic feet, or 5.93 cubic yards. At 100 lb per cubic foot, that is 16,000 lb — 8.00 tons of stone, the number a stone yard will quote on. Bagged stone would take 107 of the 1.5 cu ft bags, which is why driveways are always a bulk order. With 10% added for compaction and edge spill, order 6.52 cubic yards, about 8.80 tons — and most yards will happily round that to a 9-ton load.

Common mistakes

  • Ordering rounded gravel for a driveway base. Pea gravel and river rock roll under tires and never lock. Driveways and bases need angular crushed stone; save the round stone for decorative, low-traffic areas.
  • Calculating one blended depth for a layered build. A 4-inch base plus a 3-inch top layer is not a 7-inch layer of one product — they are different stones at different prices. Run each layer as its own area and add the results.
  • Forgetting compaction on crusher run. Minus material with fines shrinks roughly 10% when compacted. Order the loose volume with the buffer, not the finished in-place volume, or the last corner of the base ends up thin.
  • Ignoring delivery weight limits and placement. Eight tons is a real truck. Confirm the driver can reach the spot and that your surface can take the truck — a loaded stone truck on a weak asphalt drive or septic field is an expensive mistake.

Frequently asked questions

How many tons of crushed stone do I need?

Find the volume first (length × width × depth in feet ÷ 27 for cubic yards), then multiply cubic feet by 100 lb and divide by 2,000. A 12 × 40 ft driveway at 4 inches deep works out to 160 cu ft — 5.93 cubic yards, or 8.00 tons.

How much does a cubic yard of crushed stone weigh?

About 2,700 lb, or 1.35 tons, at the standard 100 lb per cubic foot average used here. Washed single-size stone can be a touch lighter; crusher run with fines is right at this figure once compacted.

What size crushed stone is best for a driveway?

A common build is a 4-inch base of large stone (around 2–3 inch, or #2) topped with #57 (about 1 inch, clean) or #411, which blends #57 with dust so it packs into a firm driving surface. On firm, flat ground a single 4-inch layer of #411 or crusher run is often enough.

What is the difference between crushed stone and gravel?

Crushed stone is machine-broken, angular rock that interlocks and compacts; gravel in the decorative sense (pea gravel, river rock) is naturally rounded and stays loose. Use crushed stone anywhere vehicles, pavers or structures bear on it, and rounded stone where looks and drainage matter more than lock-up.

How deep should crushed stone be under pavers?

Typically 4–6 inches of compacted crusher run or 3/4″ minus base, then a 1-inch bedding sand layer, then the pavers. Walkway patios sit at the 4-inch end; driveways and soft soils need 6 inches or more.
