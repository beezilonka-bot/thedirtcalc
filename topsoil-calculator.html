How to calculate topsoil

Topsoil is sold by volume, not by the square foot, so every calculation starts the same way: volume = area × depth. Measure the length and width of the area in feet, then decide how deep the new soil layer should be. A new lawn usually wants 4–6 inches of topsoil over rough grade; topdressing an existing lawn needs only ¼–½ inch; raised garden beds are covered by our dedicated raised bed calculator.

Multiply length × width × depth (with depth converted to feet — 4 inches is 0.33 ft) to get cubic feet, then divide by 27 to get cubic yards, the unit bulk suppliers sell by. To estimate weight, multiply cubic feet by the soil's density. Average screened topsoil runs about 75 lb per cubic foot, so one cubic yard weighs just over a ton (about 2,025 lb). That weight matters if you are hauling it yourself: a half-ton pickup can legally carry roughly half a yard of topsoil per trip.

Finally, order a little more than the math says. Soil settles and compacts after watering, grades are never perfectly flat, and spilled wheelbarrow loads add up. This calculator's recommended order adds 10%, which experience shows is the difference between finishing on Saturday and making an emergency run on Sunday. If you are filling over very uneven ground, measure depth at several spots and use the average — the deepest hollow always eats more soil than you expect.

Worked example: 20 ft × 30 ft lawn area, 4 inches deep

A 20 ft × 30 ft area is 600 sq ft. At 4 inches (0.33 ft) deep, the volume is 600 × 0.33 = 200 cubic feet, which is 200 ÷ 27 = 7.41 cubic yards. At 75 lb per cubic foot that soil weighs about 15,000 lb, or 7.50 tons — far beyond a pickup truck, so this is a delivery order. In bags it would take 134 of the 1.5 cu ft size, which is why anything over about 2 yards is almost always cheaper in bulk (see the bulk vs. bagged calculator). With the 10% buffer, order 8.15 cubic yards.

Common mistakes

  • Measuring depth in feet by accident. Entering “4” as feet instead of inches inflates the order 12-fold. This calculator takes depth in inches (or cm) on purpose.
  • Forgetting that soil settles. Freshly spread topsoil compacts 10–15% after rain and watering. Skipping the buffer leaves you visibly short.
  • Using one depth for a sloped yard. A single measurement on uneven ground can miss by half. Average several spots, or split slopes into separate areas with the “second area” option.
  • Hauling bulk soil in a small pickup. A cubic yard of topsoil weighs about a ton. Overloading a half-ton truck is a safety and suspension problem, not a money saver.

Frequently asked questions

How many bags of topsoil equal one cubic yard?

One cubic yard is 27 cubic feet. That is 36 bags at 0.75 cu ft each, or 18 bags at 1.5 cu ft each. Many big-box “topsoil” bags are 0.75 cu ft, so read the bag label before comparing prices.

How deep should topsoil be for a new lawn?

Four inches is the practical minimum over rough or compacted grade; 6 inches gives roots more room and better drought tolerance. For overseeding an existing lawn, ¼ to ½ inch of topdressing is plenty.

How much does a cubic yard of topsoil weigh?

About 2,000–2,200 lb (roughly one ton) for typical screened topsoil at average moisture. This calculator uses 2,025 lb per yard (75 lb/cu ft); saturated soil can be several hundred pounds heavier.

Should I order topsoil by the yard or by the ton?

Most landscape suppliers sell by the cubic yard for delivery and by the ton at the yard scale. The two are close for topsoil — about one ton per yard — but ask which unit the quoted price uses before comparing suppliers.

How much extra topsoil should I order?

Ten percent over the calculated volume covers settling, spills and uneven depth for most flat projects. Go to 15–20% for sloped or very rough ground.
