Quick answer

Two 4 ft × 8 ft raised beds filled 12 inches deep need 2.37 cubic yards of soil mix — about 1.44 tons, or 43 bags of 1.5 cu ft. Each single 4 × 8 bed holds 32 cubic feet (1.19 cubic yards). With the 10% settling buffer, order about 2.6 cubic yards in bulk, or 48 bags.

  • The math: volume = length × width × depth (depth in feet); cubic yards = cubic feet ÷ 27; tons = cubic feet × density ÷ 2,000.
  • Density used: 45 lb per cubic foot (0.61 tons per cubic yard) for a moist topsoil/compost raised-bed blend.
  • Bag counts are rounded up and given for both standard bag sizes — 0.75 cu ft and 1.5 cu ft (one cubic yard = 36 bags of 0.75 cu ft, or 18 bags of 1.5 cu ft).
  • The recommended order adds 10% on top of the calculated volume for settling, compaction and spills.

How much soil does a raised bed hold?

A raised bed is a simple box, so the math is the friendliest on this site: length × width × depth, all in feet, equals cubic feet. A standard 4 ft × 8 ft bed filled 12 inches deep holds 4 × 8 × 1 = 32 cubic feet — about 1.19 cubic yards. Enter the inside dimensions of the frame, not the outside, and measure depth to where you actually want the soil line, usually an inch or two below the rim to keep mulch and soil from washing over the edge.

Bag shopping is where beds surprise people. Soil mix is commonly sold in 1.5 cu ft bags, so that single 4×8 bed needs about 22 bags; two beds need 43. At typical big-box prices that is often two to three times the cost of a bulk yard of blended garden soil delivered. The crossover is roughly one cubic yard: below it, bags win on convenience; above it, bulk usually wins on price. The bulk vs. bagged calculator on this site will run your local numbers exactly.

Two practical adjustments are built into the recommendation. First, loose mix fluffs up in the bag and settles once watered — expect a freshly filled bed to drop an inch or more in the first weeks, which the 10% buffer covers. Second, deep beds (18 inches or more) do not need to be all premium mix: many gardeners fill the bottom third with plain topsoil or even coarse organic material and save the good mix for the top 10–12 inches where roots feed. Use the second-area option to calculate a deep bed's two layers separately if you go that route.

Worked example: two 4 ft × 8 ft beds, 12 inches deep

Each 4 ft × 8 ft bed filled 12 inches (1 ft) deep holds 4 × 8 × 1 = 32 cubic feet. Two beds need 64 cubic feet = 2.37 cubic yards. In 1.5 cu ft bags that is 43 bags — a strong hint to price a bulk delivery of garden mix instead. The mix weighs about 64 × 45 = 2,880 lb (1.44 tons) total, which is also why you fill beds in place rather than moving them after. With the settling buffer, order about 2.6 cubic yards in bulk or 48 bags.

Common mistakes

  • Measuring the outside of the frame. Lumber thickness steals real volume — a “4×8” bed built from 2× lumber is closer to 3 ft 9 in × 7 ft 9 in inside. Small, but it adds up across several beds.
  • Filling to the very rim. Soil at rim level washes out with the first heavy watering. Leave 1–2 inches of headroom; your depth entry should match the soil line, not the frame height.
  • Buying bagged mix for a whole garden of beds. Past about one cubic yard total, bulk blended soil is usually dramatically cheaper per cubic foot — and produces a lot less plastic waste.
  • Ignoring settling. Fresh mix can settle 10% or more after a few waterings. The buffer in this calculator exists so your beds do not end the season under-filled.

Frequently asked questions

How many bags of soil fill a 4×8 raised bed?

A 4×8 bed filled 12 inches deep holds 32 cu ft: about 22 bags at 1.5 cu ft, or 43 bags at 0.75 cu ft. At 8 inches deep it holds about 21 cu ft — roughly 15 bags at 1.5 cu ft.

How much soil do I need for a 4×4 bed?

At 12 inches deep, 16 cu ft (about 11 bags of 1.5 cu ft). At 10 inches deep, about 13.3 cu ft. Use the 4×4 preset above for exact numbers at your depth.

What is the best soil mix ratio for raised beds?

A common recipe is about 60% topsoil, 30% compost and 10% aeration material (perlite, coarse sand or fine bark). Pre-mixed “raised bed soil” bags follow roughly this blend, which is what this calculator's density assumes.

Should I fill the whole bed with premium mix?

Roots feed mostly in the top 10–12 inches. For beds deeper than about 14 inches, filling the bottom with plain topsoil (calculated separately with the topsoil calculator) cuts cost without hurting plants.

How often do raised beds need topping up?

Expect to add ½–1 inch of compost or mix each year as organic matter decomposes and the level drops. A 4×8 bed needs about 2.7 cu ft — two 1.5 cu ft bags — per inch of top-up.
