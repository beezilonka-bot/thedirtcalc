How to calculate dirt for a project

“Dirt” is the word most people use for the plain mineral soil that goes into holes and under things — backfill against a foundation, fill for a removed pool or pond, or the base layer for levelling a yard before topsoil goes on top. The volume math is the same as for any material: length × width × depth ÷ 27 = cubic yards. For a round hole or an old above-ground pool ring, use the circle shape and enter the diameter. Dirt is normally sold by the cubic yard for delivery and weighed by the ton at the scale, and at roughly 1.22 tons per yard the two units are close but not interchangeable — confirm which one your quote uses.

What makes dirt different from the growing materials on this site is compaction. Loose dirt dumped into a hole settles as it is rained on and packed down — typically 10–15% for hand-placed fill, more if it goes in dry and fluffy. Practical rule: figure the true volume of the space, then order about 10–15% extra loose dirt to end up level after settling. This calculator's recommended order builds in 10%; use the depth field to add a little more for deep fills that will only be settled by weather.

One distinction worth knowing before you order: dirt is not topsoil. Topsoil is the screened, organic top layer plants need. The common pattern for a yard rebuild is dirt first to set the grade, then 4–6 inches of topsoil over it to grow in. If your project is mostly filling and shaping, this page is the right one; if it is mostly growing, use the topsoil calculator for the top layer and this one for everything underneath.

Worked example: levelling a 30 ft × 40 ft yard section, 6 inches average depth

A 30 ft × 40 ft area is 1,200 sq ft. At an average depth of 6 inches (0.5 ft) — realistic for a yard with real humps and hollows — the volume is 1,200 × 0.5 = 600 cubic feet, or 22.22 cubic yards. At 90 lb per cubic foot that is about 54,000 lb, or 27.00 tons — several dump-truck loads, so have it delivered and dumped close to where it will be spread. In bags the same job would take 400 of the 1.5 cu ft size, which is why nobody bags a levelling job. With the 10% settling buffer, order 24.44 cubic yards.

Common mistakes

  • Ordering the exact hole volume, then watching it sink. Uncompacted fill settles 10–15%. A backfilled trench or pool hole that starts dead level will be a visible dip by the end of the season without the extra material (and ideally tamping in layers).
  • Buying dirt to grow grass in. Subsoil has almost no organic matter or structure; seed spread on it struggles. Grade with dirt, then finish with 4–6 inches of real topsoil where anything needs to grow.
  • Using one depth measurement on rough ground. Depth for levelling is an average, not a single reading. Measure the hollows, average several spots, and expect the deepest areas to eat far more than the average suggests.
  • Ignoring the weight. At about 1.22 tons per yard, even a modest 5-yard fill is a 6-ton load. That is delivery-truck territory; trying to move it in a half-ton pickup means many trips and an overloaded truck.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between dirt, fill dirt and topsoil?

They are layers of the same ground. Topsoil is the dark, organic top layer plants grow in. Fill dirt is plain subsoil used to build up grade and fill holes. “Dirt” is the everyday word covering both, but when a supplier sells you dirt it is almost always subsoil fill — fine for shaping, poor for growing.

How much dirt do I need to fill a hole?

Treat the hole as a box or cylinder: length × width × depth ÷ 27 gives cubic yards (pick the circle shape here for a round hole and enter its diameter). Then add 10–15% because loose fill settles. A 15 ft diameter pool hole 4 ft deep, for example, needs about 26 cubic yards before the settling allowance.

How much does a cubic yard of dirt weigh?

About 2,430 lb (1.22 tons) for typical subsoil dirt at average moisture — this calculator uses 90 lb per cubic foot. Wet, clay-heavy dirt can approach 2,700 lb per yard; dry sandy dirt can be a few hundred pounds lighter.

Will dirt settle after I fill a hole?

Yes — loose fill typically settles 10–15% as rain and gravity compact it, and more if it was placed dry. Tamping in 6-inch layers as you fill cuts settling dramatically. Either way, mound deep fills slightly high or keep extra dirt on hand for a top-up pass.

Can I put topsoil straight over dirt fill?

Yes, that is the standard sequence: dirt sets the shape, topsoil grows the plants. Give the fill a rain or two (or tamp it) before spreading topsoil so the finished surface does not sink unevenly, then add 4–6 inches of topsoil where grass or beds will go.
