How much sand does a paver patio need?

A paver installation is a sandwich, and sand appears in two different layers that are bought and calculated separately. The bedding layer is a bed of coarse sand screeded to exactly 1 inch over the compacted gravel base; the pavers sit in it, and final compaction settles them about ⅜ inch into it. Its volume is patio area × 1 inch, which is what the calculator above computes at the standard sand density of 100 lb per cubic foot — use it for the tons or yards to order. The joint sand is swept over the finished surface and vibrated down into the gaps between pavers, locking the field together; that is the second calculator on this page.

Two rules protect the whole project. First, the bedding layer's thickness must be uniform, not generous: a 2-inch bedding layer feels safer and performs worse, because thick sand shifts and pavers rock and settle unevenly. Screed it at 1 inch, lay the pavers, and leave the fine-tuning to the compaction pass. Second, bedding sand goes over a properly built base — typically 4–6 inches of compacted crushed stone (the crushed stone calculator sizes that layer).

For joint sand, the amount depends on how much of your patio is gap. With standard 4 × 8 inch pavers and ⅛-inch joints, the joints are only about 4.5% of the surface — but they run the full 2⅜-inch thickness of the paver, so a mid-size patio still takes several 50 lb bags. Wider joints or thicker pavers push the number up quickly, which is why the joint calculator asks for paver size and joint width rather than guessing. Polymeric sand — joint sand with a binder that sets when misted — is the default choice for patios because it resists washout and weeds; it is sold in 50 lb bags, and the joint calculator reports whole bags.

Common mistakes

  • Making the bedding layer thicker “for safety”. Bedding sand must be a uniform 1 inch. Extra depth does not add strength — it adds movement, and pavers settle unevenly into it.
  • Using play sand or masonry sand as bedding. Fine, round, single-size grains roll instead of locking. Bedding wants coarse, angular, washed concrete sand; the wrong sand is the most common cause of a patio that will not stay flat.
  • Skipping or skimping the joint sand. Empty joints let pavers creep, rock and chip at the edges, and invite weeds and ants. Sweep, compact, sweep again — the joints should end up full to just below the paver surface.
  • Soaking polymeric sand instead of misting it. Drowning the surface washes the binder out of the joints and stains the pavers. Light misting passes, per the bag instructions, activate it without moving it.

Frequently asked questions

How much bedding sand do I need under pavers?

One inch over the whole patio: area in sq ft ÷ 12 = cubic feet. A 12 × 16 ft patio (192 sq ft) needs 16 cu ft, about 0.59 cubic yards or 0.80 tons. Order the recommended amount with the 10% buffer for screeding losses and edge spill.

How much joint sand do I need?

It depends on paver size, joint width and paver thickness. For 4 × 8 in pavers with ⅛ in joints at 2⅜ in thick, expect roughly one 50 lb bag per 45–55 sq ft of patio — a 192 sq ft patio takes about 4 bags. Use the joint sand calculator on this page for your exact format.

What kind of sand goes under pavers?

Coarse, washed concrete sand (sometimes labelled bedding sand) — angular grains that lock together. Not play sand, not beach sand, not fine masonry sand: those are too round and uniform, and the paver field will keep shifting on them.

Can I use regular sand instead of polymeric sand in the joints?

You can — plain jointing sand is cheaper and is what older patios used — but it washes out, blows away and grows weeds, so it needs topping up regularly. Polymeric sand sets after misting and resists all three, which is why it is the default recommendation for patios and walkways.

How thick should the gravel base under the sand be?

Typically 4–6 inches of compacted crushed stone (crusher run or 3/4″ minus) under the 1-inch sand layer: 4 inches for a walkway patio on firm soil, 6 or more for driveways and soft or clay soils.
